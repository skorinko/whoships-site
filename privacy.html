Privacy Policy

Effective 19 November 2026

In short: Whoships reads your GitHub organization's data from GitHub and shows it on your Mac. Your GitHub data and sign-in never leave your Mac. The App Store version sends nothing to the developer. The version downloaded from whoships.app sends an anonymous "app opened" signal at most once a day, which you can turn off. This website counts visits without cookies. If you buy on whoships.app, Paddle processes the payment and we receive your name and email to send your license key.

Who we are

Whoships is made by Dmitry Skorinko, a sole trader based in Spain. It is sold on the Mac App Store and on whoships.app.

What the app reads

When you sign in, Whoships uses GitHub's API on your behalf to read the organization you choose: pull requests, reviews, checks and Actions runs, branches and pushes, members and teams, and, where your access allows, security alerts and billing usage. It only reads; it never changes anything on GitHub.

Where your data goes

Nowhere but your Mac. Whoships connects to:

  • api.github.com and github.com, to sign you in and read your organization's data;
  • githubstatus.com, to read GitHub's public service status.

The version downloaded from whoships.app also connects to whoships.app to check for updates and to send anonymous usage statistics (see below). The developer never receives your GitHub data, your sign-in or what you look at in Whoships.

What the app stores

Your GitHub sign-in (an access token) is kept in your Mac's keychain. Your settings and the latest results the app fetched are kept in the app's own container on your Mac, so the widgets can show them. Signing out deletes the token and the fetched results. Deleting the app deletes everything it stored.

Notifications

If you allow them, Whoships shows macOS notifications for new urgent items. They are created on your Mac and are not sent through any server.

Sample data

"Try with sample data" shows a made-up organization. It reads nothing from GitHub and needs no account.

GitHub

Your use of GitHub is governed by GitHub's own terms and privacy statement. You can review or revoke Whoships's access at any time in your GitHub settings under Applications.

Purchases on the Mac App Store

An App Store purchase is handled entirely by Apple. The developer does not receive your name, email address or payment details. Apple's privacy policy applies to the purchase.

Purchases on whoships.app

Purchases on this website are processed by Paddle.com, our reseller and Merchant of Record, under Paddle's Privacy Policy. Paddle collects your payment details; we never see them. Paddle shares with us your name, email address, country and order details, which we use only to send your license key, provide support, handle refunds and meet tax and legal obligations. We keep order records for as long as the law requires.

Your license key contains the email address you bought with. When you enter it, Whoships checks it on your Mac and stores it in your Mac's keychain. The key is not sent to us when you use the app.

Update checks (whoships.app version)

The version downloaded from whoships.app checks for updates by downloading a list of releases from whoships.app, like loading a web page. The request includes your IP address and the app's version, as any web request does. It doesn't include your GitHub data, settings or license. You can turn automatic checks off. The App Store version is updated by the App Store.

Usage statistics (whoships.app version)

So we know how many people use Whoships, the version downloaded from whoships.app sends a short message to whoships.app when it is first opened, and at most once a day after that. It contains a random ID created on your Mac (not linked to your name, email, GitHub account or license), the app and macOS versions, and whether the app is unlocked. It never contains your GitHub data, your organization, your settings or anything you type. Your approximate location (country and city) is taken from the connection; your IP address is not stored. Turn it off in Settings › General › Share anonymous usage statistics. The App Store version sends none of this.

To tell which website or ad led to a download, whoships.app keeps, for up to 48 hours, a code derived from the downloading network's IP address with a secret that changes every day, together with the link the download came from. If the app's first message comes from the same network, it is credited to that link. The code can't be turned back into an IP address and is deleted after 48 hours. When you open the buy page from the app, the link names only that source (like "reddit"), never the random ID.

This website

whoships.app uses no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. To learn which links bring visitors, it records the pages you view, where you came from (the referring site and campaign tags in the link) and clicks on the download, pricing and buy buttons. These statistics are processed for us by PostHog in the EU. Your IP address is used to work out your approximate location and to count unique visitors per day, and is not kept in the statistics. A purchase is counted with the link that brought the buyer, its amount and country, never the buyer's name or email. The site runs through Cloudflare, which protects it and may process your IP address under Cloudflare's Privacy Policy. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may log visitors' IP addresses for security under GitHub's Privacy Statement. When you start a checkout, Paddle's checkout runs on this site and may set its own cookies.

Children

Whoships collects no personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
